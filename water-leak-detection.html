Tools and methods

Tools and methods we use to find leaks

01

Sewerin acoustic leak detector

Picks up the sound of pressurized water escaping under a slab or behind a wall. We use it to narrow the leak to within a few inches before any access is opened.

02

Fluke thermal imaging camera

Shows temperature differences caused by escaping water. Ideal for finding leaks behind tile, under carpet, and inside wall cavities where sound is muffled.

03

Pressure testing kit

Isolates and pressurizes the supply system to confirm whether the leak is on the pressurized side or the drain side. This test drives the entire inspection plan.

04

General Pipe Cleaners video pipe inspection camera

Runs through sewer and drain lines to find root intrusion, cracked PVC pipe, offset joints, and bellies. We record the footage and share it with you.

05

Ultrasonic leak detector

Detects high-frequency sound from small gas and water leaks that the human ear cannot hear. Used on gas lines and compressed systems as well as water.

06

Tracer gas leak detector

Introduces a safe tracer gas into the line and sniffs for it at the surface. Useful for buried lines where acoustic sound is too faint to isolate.

07

Moisture meter

Confirms elevated moisture in drywall, wood, and concrete. We use it to verify the leak's path and to check that the area dries after repair.

08

Isolation pressure testing

We shut off fixtures and irrigation, then pressurize the domestic system. A dropping gauge confirms a supply leak; a holding gauge redirects us to drains or fixtures. This is the first step on every job because it eliminates half the possibilities in ten minutes.

09

Acoustic correlation

Two sensors placed along the suspected line pick up the leak sound and a processor calculates the exact point between them. It is the same principle used on municipal water mains, scaled down for residential copper pipe and PEX tubing.

10

Thermal mapping

We scan the affected area with a thermal imaging camera and compare it to a baseline reading. Escaping hot water shows as a warm streak; escaping cold water shows as a cool one. The map tells us where to open and where not to.

11

Video pipe inspection

A camera on a reel travels the drain or sewer line and sends live video to a monitor. We look for root intrusion, cracked PVC pipe, offset joints, and corrosion. You see the same footage we do, which makes repair decisions straightforward.

Problems we solve

Common water leak problems we solve in Redlands

Most calls fall into four categories. Each one has a different cause and a different repair path, which is why proper detection matters before anyone picks up a wrench.

01

Water bill jumped with no visible leak

We pressure test the supply system, then use acoustic and thermal gear to find the exact leak point. You get a marked location and a repair plan the same day, so the waste stops fast. A hidden pinhole leak or slab leak can waste 10,000 gallons a month and add $150 or more to your bill while quietly softening the soil under your foundation.

02

Warm spot or cracked tile on the floor

We confirm the slab leak with thermal imaging and acoustic listening, then map the line so repair opens the smallest possible area. Most slab repairs need a two-foot access point, not a full floor tear-out. A hot-water slab leak heats the floor above it and expands the slab, cracking tile and drywall. Left alone, it can undermine the foundation and cost five figures to repair.

03

Pool losing more than two inches a week

We pressure test the pool plumbing, dye test the shell and light niches, and check the skimmer. You get a clear answer on whether it is a real leak or normal evaporation, plus a repair estimate. A pool line leak wastes thousands of gallons and can undermine the deck and shell. Many homeowners mistake it for evaporation and lose a full season before calling.

04

Soggy yard or sewer smell near the line

We run a video pipe inspection camera through the line to find root intrusion, cracked PVC pipe, or offset joints. Then we recommend spot repair or line replacement based on what the camera shows. A sewer line leak or root intrusion creates a wet patch, odor, and slow drains. Ignored, it can collapse the line and cost thousands in excavation.
