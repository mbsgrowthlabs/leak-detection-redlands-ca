How to tell if you have a slab leak in Redlands

Warm floors, unexplained water bills, or a meter that never stops spinning? Here's how to tell if you have a slab leak in Redlands and what to do next.

Leak Detection Redlands CAOctober 20269 min read

You have a slab leak in Redlands if your water meter keeps spinning with every fixture off, you feel warm spots on the floor, or you hear running water when nothing is on. Those three signs together are almost never a coincidence. This post walks through the full list of slab leak symptoms Redlands homeowners report, how to confirm the problem yourself in about twenty minutes, and when it is time to call a pro.

What a slab leak actually is

How to tell if you have a slab leak in Redlands is really a question about water escaping from the pipes that run underneath your foundation. A slab leak is a failure in a pressurized supply line or drain line that sits below or inside your concrete slab, and it needs attention the moment you suspect it. Most Redlands homes built after World War II sit on post-tension or conventional slabs, and the copper supply lines running through them are now 40 to 70 years old.

Two things happen to copper in that environment. It corrodes from the outside because Southern California soil shifts and stays slightly alkaline, and it erodes from the inside because Redlands water carries dissolved minerals that slowly wear the pipe wall. When the wall finally gives, water does not spray - it seeps. That is why slab leaks are so easy to miss for weeks.

If you want the short version: a slab leak is water leaving a pipe under your floor and soaking the soil beneath your foundation. Left alone, it undermines the slab, warps flooring, and grows mold. Our team handles Slab Leak Detection Redlands CA every week, and the pattern is always the same - the homeowner noticed something small months ago and talked themselves out of it.

The signs Redlands homeowners notice first

Slab leak warning signs Redlands CA residents report fall into four groups: sound, temperature, water, and money. Start with sound, because it is the easiest to check.

Sounds you should not be hearing

Put your ear near the floor in a quiet room. A faint hiss or the sound of running water when every tap, appliance, and irrigation zone is off points to a pressurized line leaking under the slab. In older homes near Leak Detection in Downtown Redlands, we often hear this under original hardwood or tile in hallways that run the length of the house - that is where the main supply line usually sits.

Warm spots and hot-floor mysteries

If you have a slab leak on a hot water line, the floor above it will feel warm to the touch even when the heater is off. Walk barefoot across the house in the morning and pay attention. A warm strip two to three feet wide is a strong signal. This is one of the most reliable slab leak symptoms Redlands homeowners describe, and it is also one of the easiest to dismiss as "the sun hitting the floor."

Water where it should not be

  • Damp or buckling flooring - tile that pops loose, laminate that cups at the seams, carpet that stays wet after a dry week.
  • Baseboards that swell, peel, or show a dark waterline.
  • A musty smell in one room that never goes away, even with the windows open.
  • Cracks in the slab or in tile grout that appear or widen over a few months.
  • Mold or mildew along the bottom of a wall that backs up to a bathroom or kitchen.

The water bill that will not behave

Compare your last three bills. A slab leak typically adds 2,000 to 10,000 gallons a month to a household that has not changed its habits. In Redlands, where summer irrigation already pushes usage up, the leak often hides inside the seasonal increase. If your bill jumped 30 percent or more with no change in watering, that is your cue to test the meter.

How to confirm a slab leak yourself

How to detect a slab leak Redlands homeowners can do without tools takes about twenty minutes. Follow these steps in order.

  1. Shut everything off. Close every faucet, turn off the ice maker, the dishwasher, the washing machine, and all irrigation zones. Make sure no toilet is running.
  2. Find your meter. Most Redlands homes have the meter in a concrete box near the curb. Lift the lid and wipe the face clean.
  3. Watch the leak dial. The small triangle or star on the meter face spins whenever water moves through it. If it spins with everything off, you have a leak somewhere on the supply side.
  4. Wait fifteen minutes. Note the reading on the main dial, wait, and read it again. Any movement confirms a leak.
  5. Isolate the house. Close the main shutoff valve at the house. If the meter stops spinning, the leak is inside your plumbing - and if the warm-floor test was positive, it is under the slab.

That last step is the one most people skip, and it is the one that separates a slab leak from a leaky irrigation valve or a running toilet. If the meter keeps spinning with the house valve closed, the problem is between the meter and the house.

For a leak you cannot pin down by ear or by meter, we use acoustic listening equipment and thermal imaging to map the pipe under the slab before anyone picks up a hammer. That is the difference between a targeted repair and a random one.

Why slab leaks are common in Redlands

Slab leaks are common in Redlands because the housing stock is old, the soil moves, and the water is hard. Three factors stack up.

First, age. A large share of Redlands homes were built between 1920 and 1975, which means the original copper under the slab is well past its expected service life of 50 years. Second, soil. The expansive clay and decomposed granite under much of the city expands when it gets wet and shrinks when it dries, and that cycle flexes the pipe every season. Third, water chemistry. Redlands municipal water runs moderately hard, and hard water slowly erodes copper from the inside.

Neighborhood matters too. In Leak Detection in North Redlands, where many homes date to the 1950s and 1960s, we see more hot-water slab leaks because the original copper lines were thinner. Downtown, the mix of 1920s bungalows and mid-century remodels means we often find a slab leak next to plumbing that was replaced ten years ago - the new pipe is fine, the old run beside it is not.

What to watch out for

The biggest mistake Redlands homeowners make with a suspected slab leak is waiting. Water under a slab does not stop. It travels along the path of least resistance, which is usually toward the lowest corner of the foundation, and it takes the soil with it.

Here is what to avoid:

  • Do not assume it is the irrigation. A leaking valve and a slab leak look identical on the meter. Isolate the house valve before you call anyone.
  • Do not pour concrete patch over a crack and call it fixed. The crack is a symptom. The pipe underneath is the problem.
  • Do not run the water heater hotter to "compensate." Higher temperature accelerates corrosion on an already failing line.
  • Do not trust a $49 "leak special" that ends in a $6,000 repair quote. Ask how the leak will be located before anyone starts work.
  • Do not ignore a musty smell. By the time you smell mold, the subfloor and often the bottom plate of the wall are already wet.

One more: if your home was built before 1980 and has never had a slab leak, that is not proof of good luck. It is proof the pipe has not failed yet. The average lifespan of copper under a Redlands slab is 40 to 60 years, and many of these lines are already past that.

When to call a professional

Call a professional the moment your meter spins with everything off and the house valve closed. That single test confirms a leak on the supply side, and if you also have warm floors or a damp baseboard, it is almost certainly under the slab.

You should also call us if:

  • You hear running water at night when the house is quiet.
  • A section of floor stays warm when the heater has been off for hours.
  • You see new cracks in tile or slab, or a baseboard that has started to swell.
  • You smell mold in a room with no obvious source.
